What germs are and why handwashing matters

Germs are tiny living things that are too small to see without a microscope.
Some germs are harmless, and some help our bodies, but others can make us sick.
These germs travel on hands, on doorknobs, and in the air when someone coughs
or sneezes. Washing hands with soap and water is one of the simplest ways to
stop germs from spreading. Soap lifts dirt and germs off the skin, and running
water rinses them away. Hands should be washed before eating, after using the
bathroom, and after touching animals. Twenty seconds of scrubbing is about as
long as it takes to hum a short song twice. Clean hands protect the person
washing them and everyone they meet afterward.
