How a light bulb works

A light bulb turns electricity into light. Inside the glass bulb is a thin
wire called a filament. When you flip the switch, electric current flows
through the filament. The filament resists the flow of electricity, and that
resistance makes it hot -- so hot that it glows. In an old-fashioned bulb, the
glowing wire gives off both light and heat. Newer bulbs, such as LEDs, work
differently: they send electricity through a special material that releases
light directly without getting nearly as hot. That is why LED bulbs use less
electricity and last much longer than the older kind. If a bulb ever breaks,
it should be swept up carefully and disposed of according to local rules.
