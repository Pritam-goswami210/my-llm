Why computers use binary

Computers do not count with the ten digits people use every day. Inside a
computer, information is stored as millions of tiny switches that can be either
on or off. Each switch holds one binary digit, or bit, which is either a zero
or a one. Groups of eight bits form a byte, and bytes combine to represent
letters, numbers, pictures, and sound. When you press a key on the keyboard,
the computer turns that letter into a pattern of bits. When you save a file,
the computer writes those patterns to storage. Binary works well because the
on-off states are easy to build and hard to confuse. A switch is either on or
it is off; there is no in-between that could cause mistakes.
