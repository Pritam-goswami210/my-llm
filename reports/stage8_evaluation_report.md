## Interpretation

### What the metrics show

- **Train/val loss:** Lower is better. A gap between train and val loss indicates overfitting.
- **Perplexity:** exp(loss). Lower is better. Roughly equals the average number of equally-likely choices the model faces at each position.
- **Vocabulary utilization:** Fraction of vocab tokens the model actually predicts. Low utilization (<30%) suggests the model has collapsed to a few frequent tokens.
- **Repetition score:** Higher values indicate repetitive, looping generation — a sign of poor sampling or a poorly trained model.

### What the metrics cannot show

- These metrics measure statistical next-character prediction, not understanding, reasoning, or factual accuracy.
- A low loss does not mean the model can answer questions correctly or maintain conversational context.
- Generation quality on 5 short prompts is not a substitute for systematic human evaluation.

### Honest assessment

A model trained for only a few dozen steps on ~200 KB of text will produce mostly gibberish. This is expected. Natural language communication requires much more data and many more training steps (Phase 9+).
