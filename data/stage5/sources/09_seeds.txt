How seeds grow

A seed holds a tiny plant inside, waiting for the right conditions. When a seed
lands in soil that is moist and warm enough, it begins to germinate. A small
root pushes downward into the soil to anchor the plant and draw up water. A
small shoot pushes upward toward the light. The first leaves that appear are
often simple and rounded. As the plant grows, it makes its own food using
sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide from the air. This process is called
photosynthesis. Without enough light, the plant grows tall and weak as it
searches for the sun. Without enough water, it wilts. Given both, a small seed
can become a tree that lives for many years.
