What the moon does

The moon is Earth's natural companion in space. It does not make its own
light; it reflects sunlight. As the moon travels around Earth, we see different
amounts of its lit side. These changing shapes are called phases. When the
whole lit side faces us, we call it a full moon. When we see only a thin
curve, it is a crescent moon. The moon also pulls on Earth's oceans, creating
the tides that rise and fall twice each day. People have visited the moon in
spacecraft, and they brought back rocks to study. On a clear night, the moon
is bright enough to cast a soft shadow on the ground.
