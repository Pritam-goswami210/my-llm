Why shadows change length

A shadow is made when an object blocks light. If the light comes from the sun,
the shadow's direction and length change through the day. In the early morning
the sun is low in the sky, so shadows stretch out long behind objects. Around
noon the sun is higher, and shadows grow short. In the afternoon the sun moves
lower again, and shadows lengthen once more. The shape of the object affects
the shadow too: a round ball makes a round shadow, while a tall pole makes a
long thin one. On a cloudy day the light is scattered, so shadows become faint
or disappear altogether. Shadows remind us that light travels in straight lines.
