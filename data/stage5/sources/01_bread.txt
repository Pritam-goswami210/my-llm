Bread, from flour to loaf

Bread starts with just four things: flour, water, salt, and yeast. Mix them
together and you get dough. Knead the dough, let it rest, and the yeast wakes
up. As the yeast feeds on the flour, it releases tiny bubbles of gas. Those
bubbles get trapped inside the dough and make it rise. When the loaf goes into
a hot oven, the heat sets the structure and turns the crust golden brown. The
smell that fills the kitchen is the result of heat changing sugars and proteins
on the surface of the bread. Fresh bread is best eaten the same day, but it can
be wrapped and kept for a few more days. Stale bread should not be thrown away;
it makes good breadcrumbs or can be soaked and cooked into a simple pudding.
